# Methodology 4 Questions

**1. What is the most interesting topic covered in your domain this quarter?**

The most interesting topic covered in my domain this quarter is how to utilize simulation
through Jupyter notebooks to teach probability concepts to students, even if they are not
proficient in Python. Not only was I able to see the professor's point of view when creating
workbooks for students, I was able to create my own notebook for possible student use in the
future. We focused on probability in particular as it is hard to visualize and comprehend when
taught on pen and paper, but showing the actual simulations through Python code may be helpful
for comprehending the complex concepts.

**2. Describe a potential investigation you would like to pursue for your Quarter 2 Project.**

For my Quarter 2 project, we would like to see if there are any actual performance differences
between traditional teaching methods and our simulation Jupyter notebook versions. In order to
conduct this experiment, we would need to randomly select two groups: the control group and
the treatment group. The control group would learn from the traditional method, while the treatment
group would learn the same concept through our simulated Jupyter Notebook version. This would help
us evaluate whether there is merit to this teaching style, and if it is something we should
implement in probability education in the future.

**3. What is a potential change you'd make to the approach taken in your current Quarter 1 Project?**

A potential change I would make to the approach taken in my current Quarter 1 Project would be to
include more opportunities for the students to experiment on their own with the simulations and
concepts, rather than to have to stick with the predetermined path. The only catch that we would have
to figure out is if the student is still on the right track with how they are experimenting. However,
if implemented well, we would encourage the student to deepen their understanding of the concept on 
their own terms instead of just observing the results of our simulations.

**4. What other techniques would you be interested in using in your project?**

I would be interested in seeing if there are any other techniques other than simulation that may help
students with understanding the abstractness of probability. It may differ from concept to concept,
but I believe there may be different types of visualizations or interactive sections that may help.
Currently, my exercises mainly compose of multiple choice questions and fill in the blank coding exercises,
but I wonder if there are more interactive options or deeper learning opportunities. Particularly, I'm
concerned about how multiple choice questions are prone to being easily guessed rather than figured out.
